# Wishit

## Product purpose

Wishit answers one question: **if I buy this, how much longer do I wait for the
things I actually want?**

It is a personal money model, not a budgeting app and not an expense tracker
that happens to draw charts. You describe your income, what your month costs,
what you owe, and what you are saving for. Then you put something you want in
front of it and the app tells you what that purchase costs you in *time*: the
emergency fund that lands three months later, the trip that slips past its
target date.

Everything else in the product exists to make that answer trustworthy.

## Register

**product** — the design serves the numbers. Nobody visits Wishit to admire it;
they visit to decide something and leave.

## Users

One person managing their own money in India, salaried, in their twenties or
thirties. Comfortable with numbers, not with spreadsheets. Has EMIs, has a
wishlist, has a gap between what they intend to save and what they save.

Two modes of use, and the design has to serve both:

- **The decision session.** Ten minutes, at a desk, usually at night, weighing a
  purchase. Dense, comparative, willing to read.
- **The daily log.** Fifteen seconds, on a phone, one-handed, straight after
  paying for something. If it takes longer than not doing it, it does not happen.

## Tone

Plain and exact. The app states figures and what they mean, never congratulates,
never scolds. "₹15,000 short of the ₹16,667 this needs. At this rate it lands
about 4.2 months late" — not "You're doing great!" and not "You're behind!"

Numbers are the voice. Copy exists to say what a number means and what to do
about it.

## Strategic principles

1. **One number, everywhere the same.** Balance left is quoted in the header, on
   every page, and in the projection. If two views disagree, that is a defect,
   not a nuance.
2. **Budget plans, log records.** Projections run on budgeted figures; the
   spending log records what actually left. Neither is allowed to silently
   double-count the other.
3. **Say which evidence was used.** When the app estimates, it names its basis:
   counted at budget, extrapolated from pace, already over.
4. **Nothing is deleted quietly.** Goals are finished, not removed; months are
   recorded; money moved between goals is still your money.
5. **Never flatter.** Logging half a month must not produce a better-looking
   balance than logging none of it.

## Anti-references

- **Mint / typical PFM dashboards.** Donut charts of categories nobody acts on,
  and a "financial health score" that means nothing.
- **Bank apps.** Navy and gold, stock photography of couples on beaches,
  reassurance in place of information.
- **Crypto dashboards.** Neon on black, animated everything, urgency as decor.
- **Notion-style neutral SaaS.** Rounded cards on off-white, purple accent, so
  polite it says nothing.

## Non-goals

- Bank connections or statement scraping. Every figure is entered deliberately.
- Advice. The app shows consequences; the user decides.
- Gamification. No streaks, badges, or confetti.
